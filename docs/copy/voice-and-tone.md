# Voice and Tone Guidelines: PaperWorking Brand Narration

**Author**: Upstream Copy Strategist
**Date**: 2026-05-31
**Status**: Approved (Canonical Reference)

This document establishes the official brand voice, tone, style rules, and messaging guidelines for all public-facing copy. Every interface component, marketing block, and email template must follow these principles.

---

## 1. Brand Personality & Tone

PaperWorking talks like a **seasoned real estate operator**. We do not talk like a generic silicon valley SaaS company, nor do we write academic papers. Our tone is:
- **Direct**: We say what the product does, no filler.
- **Experienced**: We understand the exact friction of running flips and syndications (earnest money deposits, contractor delays, CPA audits).
- **Skeptical of Fluff**: We value hard, verifiable numbers over vague assertions.
- **Supportive but Firm**: We build tools that help operators look professional, and our copy reflects that authority.

---

## 2. Style Directives

1. **Use Contractions**: Keep it conversational. Write "you'll", "it's", "don't", and "can't".
2. **Speak Directly to the Operator**: Address the reader as "you" and refer to their business as "your deal" or "your portfolio".
3. **Be Concrete & Specific**: Avoid adjectives like "powerful" or "intuitive." Instead, explain *how* it works (e.g., "logs receipts directly to the property's cost basis").
4. **Sentence Variety**: Mix short, punchy statements with descriptive explanations.
5. **Verb-First Instructions**: When guiding users in the UI, use active verbs. Write "Upload your HUD-1," "Select exit path," or "Log materials draw."

## 3. Correct vs. Incorrect Examples

### 3.1 Value Propositions

- **❌ Incorrect (Vague Fluff)**:
  > *"PaperWorking is a robust, cutting-edge platform that empowers syndicators to seamlessly leverage data-driven insights and streamline their workflows."*
- **✅ Correct (Concrete & Benefit-Driven)**:
  > *"PaperWorking tracks every document, dollar, and deadline from purchase to exit. Give your investors clean reports and your CPA exact numbers without rebuilding a spreadsheet."*

---

### 3.2 Feature Headings

- **❌ Incorrect (Feature Jargon)**:
  > *"Utilize our state-of-the-art rehab management module to ensure project timelines remain aligned."*
- **✅ Correct (Action Jargon)**:
  > *"Track contractor draws, scope changes, and materials receipts in one visual checklist."*

---

### 3.3 Call to Actions (CTAs)

- **❌ Incorrect (Pressure & Friction)**:
  > *"Sign up now to immediately scale your portfolio!"* (Too salesy)
  > *"Submit Billing Information to Begin"* (Adds anxiety)
- **✅ Correct (Low Friction & Clear Value)**:
  > *"Start Free Trial"*
  > *"Try PaperWorking Free for 14 Days — No Credit Card Required"* (Underlines trial anticipation)

---

### 3.4 Banned Words vs. Allowed Substitutes

Avoid marketing fluff at all costs. Refer to this index when writing or auditing copy:

| Banned Jargon | Preferred Substitute | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `robust` | `exact`, `complete` | "Robust" has lost all meaning. |
| `streamline` | `track`, `organize` | Too generic. Tell the user *how* it organizes. |
| `leverage` | `use` | Pure corporate speak. |
| `seamlessly` | (Remove entirely) | Overused adverb that adds zero value. |
| `cutting-edge` | `verified`, `built-in` | Proves quality by evidence, not adjectives. |
| `holistic` | `full-lifecycle` | Keep it relevant to real estate. |
| `empower` | `let`, `allow` | The tool is a helper, the operator is the hero. |
| `navigate` | `go to`, `manage` | Avoid navigational metaphors. |
| `institutional-grade` | `CPA-ready`, `professional` | Keeps the promise realistic and grounded. |
